# Copy voice

One person writes this site: someone who knows the clothes, likes them, and is not trying to sell
you a lifestyle.

## Register

Quiet, specific, slightly dry. Confident without volume. The brand says *effortless everyday
luxury* — the copy must actually be effortless. If a sentence is doing work to sound luxurious, cut
it.

South Asian English, Pakistani context. `Dupatta`, `shalwar`, `kurta`, `chikankari`, `lawn`,
`karandi` are the right words — do not translate them into approximations like "scarf" or "tunic".

## Case system

This is structural, not decorative:

| Element | Case | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Campaign headline | UPPERCASE, 1–3 words | `VELVETS` · `AFTER HOURS` |
| Page title | UPPERCASE, 1–3 words | `THE ATELIER` · `NEW IN` |
| Supporting line | lowercase sentence | `your winter uniform, sorted` |
| Product name | UPPERCASE, `[COLOUR OR MOTIF] [CUT]` | `BLUSH BAGGY` · `SUNFLOWER IVORY` |
| Section label | uppercase, caption size, tracked | `FALL 2026` |
| Body, buttons, forms | Sentence case | `Message us on WhatsApp` |

Do not flatten this. A campaign headline in sentence case and a body paragraph in Title Case are
both mistakes.

## Naming

The brand names pieces `[COLOUR OR MOTIF] [CUT]`, in caps. Two parts, not three:

`BLUSH BAGGY` · `LAVENDER VANILLA` · `SUNFLOWER IVORY` · `LILAC TIE & DYE`

- **Colour or motif first.** It is what the customer sees in the photograph and searches for.
- **Cut, print or detail second.** Baggy, vanilla, ivory, tie & dye.
- **Two words wherever possible.** If a third is unavoidable, it is a garment word, not a mood.
- **Caps in the catalogue, prose elsewhere.** In a sentence: "the blush baggy is cut loose."

Never: `Women's Printed Lawn Kurta - Blue`, `BA-2026-014`, `The Marlow`, `Elevated Everyday Set`.

## Product description

40–70 words. Structure:

1. **Fabric and construction** — what it is made of, how it is finished.
2. **Cut and length** — where it falls, how it fits, what it does when worn.
3. **What is included** — dupatta, lining, separate pieces. Say it explicitly.

Example:

> Hand-embroidered chikankari on cotton lawn, finished with a scalloped hem. Falls to mid-calf with
> a straight side slit, cut generously through the body so it moves when you walk. Comes with a
> matching dupatta; the shirt is lined through the bodice.

No adjectives standing in for facts. "Luxurious fabric" says nothing; "cotton lawn, 60s count" says
everything.

## Microcopy

**Buttons** — verbs or plain nouns, never "Learn more" or "Discover":
`View the collection` · `Message us on WhatsApp` · `Size guide` · `See the full shoot` ·
`Pre-order` · `Back to shop`

**Forms** — labels above fields, sentence case. Errors explain what happened and how to fix it,
without blame:
- ✓ `Enter a phone number we can reach you on, e.g. 0300 1234567.`
- ✗ `Invalid input.`

**Empty states** — plain and short:
`Nothing here yet. The next drop lands in March.`

**Size and fit** — the highest-trust copy on the site. Be literal: `Model is 5'6" and wears a
small.` `Cut relaxed; size down for a closer fit.` Never "true to size" without a reason.

**Order handoff** — if ordering runs through WhatsApp or DM, say so plainly:
`Orders are placed over WhatsApp. Send us the piece and your size and we'll confirm availability.`

## Banned lexicon

Never ship:

*elevate · curated · timeless · discover · unleash · redefine · seamless · effortlessly chic · luxe ·
luxurious · premium · exclusive · one-of-a-kind · where X meets Y · not just a X — a Y · your new
favorite · step into · own the room · designed for the modern woman · crafted with love · a
celebration of · the art of*

Also banned:
- Exclamation marks
- "Introducing", "Say hello to", "Meet the", "The wait is over"
- Em dashes used for rhythm rather than grammar
- Three-item lists where all three are adjectives
- Any sentence that could be a perfume ad

## Rewriting an AI-sounding draft

1. Delete the first sentence. It is almost always throat-clearing.
2. Replace every abstract adjective with a concrete noun or a measurement.
3. Cut the sentence length in half.
4. Remove any sentence that describes how the customer should feel.
5. Read it aloud. If you would not say it to a friend in a shop, rewrite it.

## What good sounds like

> `good outfits. ordinary days.`
> `your winter uniform, sorted.`
> `Making an entrance comes naturally.`
> `Cotton lawn, hand-embroidered. Falls to the ankle. The dupatta is included.`

Notice: short, declarative, no adjectives doing the work of facts.
